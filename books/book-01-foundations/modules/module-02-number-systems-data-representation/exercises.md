# Exercise Set — Number Systems & Data Representation

## Easy
- [ ] Convert decimal 25 to binary.
- [ ] Convert binary `1010` to decimal.
- [ ] Convert decimal 15 to hexadecimal.
- [ ] How many bits are in 3 bytes?
- [ ] Write the ASCII decimal value for the character `A` (research or recall: 65) and convert it to binary.

---

## Medium
- [ ] Convert decimal 500 to binary, then to hexadecimal.
- [ ] Add the binary numbers `1101` and `1011` (show your work in binary, then verify by converting both to decimal first).
- [ ] Convert the hex color `#3498DB` into its individual R, G, B decimal values.
- [ ] A file is 2 MB. How many bytes is that? Show the calculation using powers of 2.
- [ ] Explain, in your own words, why UTF-8 needs a variable number of bytes per character while ASCII always uses one.

---

## Hard
- [ ] Convert decimal 987 to binary, octal, and hexadecimal — all three, showing your work.
- [ ] Subtract binary `1100` from `1011` — if the result would be negative, explain what that means and how computers actually handle negative binary numbers (research: two's complement).
- [ ] A webpage loads a 4.5 MB image over a connection that transfers 750 KB/s. Roughly how many seconds will it take to load? (Careful with your units.)
- [ ] Explain why the character `é` might take more than 1 byte to store in UTF-8, while `e` takes only 1.

---

## Bonus
- [ ] Research and explain, in 3–5 sentences, what "endianness" (big-endian vs. little-endian) means and why it matters when computers exchange binary data.
